## Users

**Primary: donors/supporters, donor-facing first (confirmed by the user).**
Someone moved by the founder's survivor story who is deciding whether to give
toward mastectomy-supply access for underserved women in Kenya. The homepage
is tuned for this decision.

**Secondary: mastectomy survivors and caregivers** (in Kenya and the
diaspora) seeking supply access or support. Reachable in one click, but not
who the homepage primarily serves.

**Referenced but unconfirmed as a designed audience:** individuals/partners
who supply or facilitate donations in kind (e.g. a US store willing to
gather donated supplies) — appears in the founder's story as precedent, not
yet confirmed as a site-served role.

## Product Purpose

Move a visitor who is moved by the founder's story to donate, funding
mastectomy supplies (external breast prostheses, mastectomy bras, related
garments) for underserved breast cancer survivors in Kenya. **Primary CTA:
Donate.** Success is a completed donation via the third-party donation
platform (not yet selected — see Capabilities and Constraints).

## Positioning

**Vision:** "Daring, Deserving and Dignified." **Mission:** "Inspiring
mastectomy survivors to walk with confidence and dignity."

The mechanism a neighboring generic breast-cancer charity could not
truthfully copy: the founder is herself a mastectomy survivor (diagnosed
2018, single mastectomy) who personally lived the exact supply gap the
foundation now exists to close — an ill-fitting knitted prosthesis that
shrank over time, no local retailer, one known shop with unaffordable
prices, and no awareness of where to even look. This is not a
general-awareness cancer charity; it targets a specific, under-served niche —
post-mastectomy garment and prosthesis access in Kenya — and fills a real,
dated void left by a named informal supplier (a US missionary based in
Mombasa, since deceased) who used to fill this need for Kenyan survivors
before her death.

## Operating Context

- **Origin story is real, personal, and dated:** 2018 diagnosis and single
  mastectomy → years of discomfort with an ill-fitting knitted prosthesis →
  introduction to a US missionary in Mombasa (a breast cancer survivor
  herself) who annually sold prostheses, bras, and swimwear and taught
  proper-fit criteria → the missionary's death from a cancer recurrence,
  leaving a supply gap → a 2023 supply trip to a mastectomy-supply store in
  Raleigh, NC with friend/sister Nneka Hay, where the store owner agreed to
  gather donations → the foundation idea born from that moment.
- **The gap this foundation fills:** mastectomy supplies are scarce and
  often unaffordable in Kenya. Without them, women often resort to makeshift
  substitutes (old cloth, a shrunken knitted prosthesis), which are
  uncomfortable, ill-fitting, and can cause visible, public embarrassment —
  described in the founder's own account of her late mother's experience as
  well as her own.
- **The essential elements of a proper post-mastectomy bra**, per the
  founder's own education from the Mombasa missionary, are real, authentic
  content usable in an educational/resources section: soft seams; a wide
  underband that sits flat on the chest between the cups; deep front and
  side panels; full cups; wide, elasticized, adjustable straps; no
  underwire.
- **Faith context runs through the founder's account** (she describes
  serving as "minister for the service," a church setting for the discomfort
  she describes) — the voice can carry a faith-rooted register without
  framing the site as a church's own site.
- **No established supply chain is confirmed** — the story references
  individual/community donation drives (a Raleigh store owner gathering
  donations) rather than an existing large-scale distribution operation. Do
  not imply an operation larger than what is confirmed.

## Product Principles

1. **The founder's own survivor story is the site's proof and its emotional
   engine.** Every other message — vision, mission, the donation ask — is
   downstream of that story, not a replacement for it.
2. **Donation is the primary action.** The homepage's dominant path moves a
   moved reader to Donate; survivor-facing help-seeking information stays
   present and reachable but does not compete with the donate path for top
   billing.
3. **Never state or imply a donation is tax-deductible, or name a specific
   legal registration, until the user confirms one.**
4. **Never fabricate what isn't confirmed:** no invented birth year for Pam,
   no invented circumstances of her death, no invented statistics, no
   invented partner list, no invented donation platform or link. Mark each as
   a stand-in or TBD until real.
5. **The specificity of the niche — post-mastectomy garment and prosthesis
   access in Kenya — is the differentiator** from generic
   breast-cancer-awareness charities. Keep copy and structure anchored to
   that specific gap rather than diluted into general cancer-support
   messaging.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific accessibility standard has been established. Given the
audience includes cancer survivors — some older, some using assistive tech,
some reached over lower-bandwidth connections in Kenya — WCAG AA is a
reasonable baseline goal for new-work to apply, but it is not yet confirmed
by the user as a hard requirement.
