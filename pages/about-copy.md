### Her story — full narrative, warm-linen
Body copy is Pam's own first-person testimony, supplied verbatim by Alex

this section; replaced entirely per direct instruction). Includes one

"In 2018, I was diagnosed with breast cancer and a single mastectomy
procedure was done. After recovery, sometimes I walked bare without a
prosthesis or anything to help. After some time a dear friend gifted me one
which was knitted from an organization that brought women together and
knitted them. This was the best gift ever because it came in handy.

It was not that comfortable because the wool put inside shrank over time,
making the whole thing small. This created the embarrassing issue of one
breast being bigger than the other. It was uncomfortable and in most
occasions, I would run to the bathroom to adjust it right in the middle of a
church service or meeting. It was more problematic if I was the minister for
the service.

I became self conscious as I felt people noticed. It was always an awkward
moment for me. I saw many women in church, at the stores, in hospitals and
Drs offices in the same predicament. I remembered that my late mum struggled
with the same thing. She too battled breast cancer. She would use old
crampled clothes inside her bra. More often than not, it was not a pretty
sight. Her confidence was crashed as it made everyone aware of her state,
even if it was unspoken. At the time of my diagnosis, I was not even aware
of where I could get breast prosthesis. I never saw a shop that sold them
nor the bras to accommodate them. I was told of one shop in town but never
tried locating it because the prices were so high.

My relief came when I was introduced to a dear lady from the US. She, a
breast cancer survivor herself, was a missionary in Mombasa. Each year, she
would open her yard to sell prosthesis, bras, swimming costumes, and
anything else that a breast cancer survivor would use. I learned a lot from
her including the essential elements of a good bra post mastectomy:

* soft seams
* wide underband that sits flat on your chest between the cups
* deep front and side panels
* full cups
* wide, elasticised, adjustable straps
* no underwire.

I saved up and purchased supplies from her for a couple of years. These
supplies together with the education I received from her significantly
impacted my life. I felt more confident and comfortable in front of crowds,
and gladly introduced other women who had endured mastectomy surgery to
her. The joy on their faces after being fitted with a bra and having their
dignity restored, always blessed my heart.

Unfortunately, she had a recurrence of cancer and passed away. This really
crushed a number of ladies who depended on her. These ladies continue to
search for a supplier of these necessary supplies as there is such a
shortage in Kenya. When available, they are often unaffordable for most
women. May women are still forced to use rags as makeshift prosthesis. This
makes them easy to spot in public as their best look uneven and misshapen,
causing embarrassment and shame.

It is for these reasons that I felt the need to help in every way I can.
After going through treatment last year (2023), my friend and sister Nneka
Hay took me to a mastectomy supply store in Raleigh, North Carolina to
purchase new supplies. She approached the store owner and explained the
predicament of many women back in Kenya. God gave us favor with the store
owner and she decided to help gather some donations for us, to restore
dignity to the lives of these women.

The idea of a foundation was birthed in that moment. I know God will touch
people to give towards this noble cause to help women who are going through
this hurdle and together you will help us to Inspire mastectomy survivors to
walk with confidence and dignity just as our mission states."
